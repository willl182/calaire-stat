Abstract
The determination of a best-fit calibration curve that describes the response
of a measuring system to the value of a standard is one of the most widely
used procedures in metrology. The mathematical basis for a generalized
least-squares solution to this problem is reviewed. Examples of the
application of a software implementation of the method are presented to
illustrate the treatment of calibration problems with different uncertainty
structures for the calibration data, including correlated data. The examples
concern the calibration of analysers to measure the composition of natural
gas and the calibration of a gas flow dilutor.

6. Analysis of natural gas

6.1. Description
The price (per unit volume) at which natural gas is traded
depends on its calorific value, a measure of the quantity of
energy released when the gas is burned. The calorific value
is calculated from measured values of the composition of the
gas. Since the amount of natural gas traded is extremely large,
it is essential that the composition is determined as well as
possible with soundly evaluated uncertainties. State-of-the-
art analysis uses a gas chromatograph (GC) to determine the
amount fraction of each component species of the gas [22,23].
A feature of natural gas analysis is that all components2
are analysed in the same sample simultaneously but usually
with different chromatographic columns and detectors. The
composition of the complete mixture is then established by
combining the results for each component.
